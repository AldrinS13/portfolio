About

I notice the number that doesn't fit.

I am a data and reporting specialist from the Philippines. For ten years I have worked where operations meet numbers: first in a call center, then in workforce management, then in healthcare. This page is the longer version of how I got here and where I am going.

The story so far

  1. SAVE 01Cebu City · 2014

    Starting on the phones

    My first job was customer service at a BPO in Cebu City, answering calls for a magazine subscription account. I learned to listen closely, keep my answers clear, and stay calm when a caller was not. Two years in, I was promoted into the workforce management team.

  2. SAVE 022016 – 2020

    Finding workforce management

    I did not plan to work with data. Workforce management found me by accident. As a real-time analyst I watched schedule adherence, service levels and queues all day, and I learned how a small gap in the numbers becomes a long wait for a customer an hour later. That was the first time I saw data change what a business did next.

  3. SAVE 032020 – 2025

    Teaching it to others

    As a training specialist and then a senior training supervisor, I trained workforce management teams in the US, Canada, India, LATAM and the Philippines. I wrote SOPs that every WFM employee in the company followed, and I prepared business reviews for senior leadership. Explaining forecasting to someone in another time zone taught me to make complicated things plain.

  4. SAVE 042025 – 2026

    Building the tools myself

    In healthcare operations I moved from reading reports to building the systems behind them: a time log tracker used by about fifty health coaches, an audit tool that checks their entries against the official record, and an IT helpdesk dashboard. Most of the work was tracing a wrong number back to its cause and fixing it there.

Rules of play

How I work

  • Evidence first

    Diagnose before changing anything

    I read the formula, the cell or the log before I touch a fix. A guess can make a number look right without making it right.

  • Known answers

    Test against figures I trust

    Each phase of a tool is checked against numbers I already know are correct before the next phase starts.

  • Simple wins

    Build what a teammate can maintain

    If nobody else can follow how a tool works, it breaks the day I am away. When two builds do the same job, I choose the simpler one.

  • Write it down

    Leave a guide behind

    SOPs, runbooks and user guides mean the next person does not have to ask me. Each system in my case files shipped with one.

Character

A little more about me

I am quiet, and I tend to observe before I speak. In a meeting I am usually the one who notices the figure that does not add up. I prefer written updates, and I would rather send one clear message than a long thread.

People who have worked with me would call me a problem solver who sets a goal and follows it through. I am always learning something, usually the thing that solves the next problem in front of me.
